FIRE vs IRIS: What Replaces the IRS FIRE System?

Last verified: 31 October 2026 against IRS.gov sources listed below · General information, not tax advice. Confirm with the IRS or a qualified tax professional before you act.

IRIS (the Information Returns Intake System) replaces FIRE. FIRE closes after 3 p.m. ET on 19 November 2026, and IRIS is the only IRS system for e-filing information returns in the 2027 filing season.

Does this apply to me?

  • You e-file 1099s or related forms and want to know what is different in IRIS.
  • You have used FIRE file uploads and are wondering about the free web option.
  • You issue corrections to previously filed returns.
  • You need to explain the change to a client or colleague.

What does not change

  • If you file 10 or more information returns in total, you must e-file (rule effective from 1 January 2024). The count combines all form types across IRIS, FIRE, AIR and Form W-2 submissions.
  • You still furnish statements to recipients by the usual dates. See the 2027 deadlines.
  • You can still get a 30-day extension to file most 1099s with Form 8809 (not for Forms W-2 and 1099-NEC).

Frequently asked questions

What replaces the IRS FIRE system?

The Information Returns Intake System (IRIS). The IRS says current FIRE users must use IRIS to file tax year 2026 information returns in the 2027 filing season.

Is IRIS only for 1099s?

The IRS e-file page lists the forms IRIS accepts by processing year. Forms 8027 and 8955-SSA are added beginning with filing year 2027, per the IRIS TCC page.

Do I need new software?

Not if you use the free Taxpayer Portal. If you use software that sends files to FIRE, ask the vendor whether it supports IRIS A2A.

Is there still a Form 1096 for IRIS?

No. The IRS FAQ says you do not file a separate transmittal such as Form 1096 through IRIS. Form 1096 is only for paper filing.

What happens to corrections?

Publication 1220 says IRIS will be the only e-file system, including corrections, after 1 January 2027. The IRS had not published full correction details for FIRE-filed returns in the documents we read.
