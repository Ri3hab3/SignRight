How to Apply for an IRIS TCC (Transmitter Control Code)

Last verified: 31 October 2026 against IRS.gov sources listed below · General information, not tax advice. Confirm with the IRS or a qualified tax professional before you act.

Apply online through the IRS IRIS Application for TCC. The IRS says processing is usually quick but can take up to 45 days (Publication 5903 says the typical application is processed within 45 business days), so apply as early as you can.

Does this apply to me?

  • You want to e-file 1099-series forms yourself in the IRIS Taxpayer Portal or through your own software.
  • You only have a FIRE TCC (numeric). IRIS needs its own TCC, which starts with the letter D.
  • You are a business, transmitter or software developer. Foreign filers who cannot get a TCC in IRIS use a separate Foreign Filer TCC Registration.
  • You do not need your own TCC if a payroll provider or tax preparer submits your returns for you.

What to have ready

  • Your organization’s EIN, legal name, business type, addresses and phone numbers.
  • Responsible Officials (RO): at least two. Sole proprietors, S-corporations and single-member LLCs need at least one RO and one contact.
  • Contacts the IRS can reach about the application (at least two, or one for the single-owner structures above).
  • For every authorized user: SSN or ITIN, date of birth, citizenship status and contact details.
  • The forms you will file and the transmission method: Portal (web) or A2A (software).
  • Your role: Issuer (only your own returns), Transmitter (returns for others) or Software Developer. You cannot choose both Issuer and Transmitter.

Source: IRS Publication 5903 (Rev. 12-2024).

How the process works

  1. Every RO and AD signs in with an IRS online account. They create one if they don’t have it.
  2. Fill in every page of the IRIS Application for TCC.
  3. Every RO signs the Application Submission page with a 5-digit PIN. Status then shows “Submitted Pending Review”.
  4. The IRS runs checks and assigns TCCs. When status is “Completed”, the TCC appears on the Application Summary page. You can use the TCC before the acceptance letter arrives by post.

How long does it take?

The IRS gives three figures, so here they are side by side:

IRS sourceWhat it says
IRIS FAQsUsually completed within 24 hours, but could take up to 45 days
Publication 5903The typical application is processed within 45 business days
Publication 1220Allow 45 business days; submit by 1 November of the year before the filing deadline (that guidance is written for FIRE applications)

The calculator above uses 45 business days by default because it is the most cautious reading. We count weekdays and skip US federal holidays; the IRS does not define “business days” on these pages.

What to do next

  1. Check you need a TCC: if a provider submits for you, ask them first.
  2. Gather the items above, and make sure each Responsible Official can sign in to their IRS online account.
  3. Start the IRIS Application for TCC and follow the Publication 5903 tutorial.
  4. Use the calculator above to check your target filing date against the 45-day window.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get an IRIS TCC?

The IRS IRIS FAQs say the process is usually completed within 24 hours but could take up to 45 days. Publication 5903 says the typical application is processed within 45 business days. Plan for the longer figure.

What does an IRIS TCC look like?

It is a five-digit code that starts with the letter D and works only with IRIS, per the IRS e-file pages.

Do I need an IRIS TCC to use the free Taxpayer Portal?

Yes. The IRS says you need an IRIS TCC to use the IRIS Taxpayer Portal.

What is the difference between Issuer and Transmitter?

An Issuer only transmits returns for the company on the application. A Transmitter is a third party that transmits for its own company and other businesses. Software Developers build software that sends returns by A2A.

Can a government agency apply?

Yes. The IRS says agencies sharing one EIN can have only one IRIS Application for TCC, so coordinate inside the agency.

I only have a FIRE TCC. Is that enough?

No. The IRS tells current FIRE users to complete an IRIS Application for TCC. See our page on whether a FIRE TCC works with IRIS.
