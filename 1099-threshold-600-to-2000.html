1099 Threshold 2026: Is the $600 Rule Gone?

Last verified: 31 October 2026 against IRS.gov sources listed below · General information, not tax advice. Confirm with the IRS or a qualified tax professional before you act.

For most payments made in tax year 2026, the 1099 threshold is $2,000, not $600. The IRS says the change applies to tax years beginning after 2025 and will be adjusted for inflation beginning in 2027.

Does this apply to me?

  • You pay contractors, freelancers or attorneys and file Form 1099-NEC.
  • You pay rent, prizes or other amounts reported on Form 1099-MISC.
  • You backup withhold on a payee (that triggers reporting at any amount).
  • You wonder whether your 2025 filing used a different rule. It did: $600.

Things the threshold does not change

  • Income is still taxable. The threshold only decides whether a form is required, not whether the payee owes tax.
  • Backup withholding: you must file a 1099-NEC for any person you backup withheld from, regardless of the amount.
  • Inflation: the IRS says the $2,000 amount may be adjusted for inflation beginning in calendar year 2027.
  • State rules may differ; check your state.

What to do next

  3. Calendar the 1 February 2027 deadline.
  4. Read the official Form 1099-MISC/NEC instructions.

Frequently asked questions

Is the $600 1099 rule gone?

For Forms 1099-NEC and most 1099-MISC payments in tax year 2026, yes: the minimum is now $2,000. $600 still appears for some forms, such as 1099-S and the new 1098-VLI.

Does the new $2,000 threshold apply to 2025?

No. Tax year 2025 returns, filed in early 2026, used $600. The IRS says the increase applies to tax years beginning after 2025.

What if I paid a contractor $1,500 in 2026?

You generally need no 1099-NEC, unless you backup withheld on that person. Keep the records anyway.

Do I still need a W-9?

The IRS general instructions (Publication 1099) cover TINs and backup withholding. Collecting a W-9 is a common way to get the payee’s TIN, so it is sensible to ask before you pay.

Does the payee still report the income?

Yes. Receiving no form does not make the income tax-free.

Does this change Form 1099-K?

Not for card payments. For third party networks the rule is more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions.
