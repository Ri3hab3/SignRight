About Invoices

An invoice is a document you send a client requesting payment for goods or services delivered. Professional invoices help you get paid on time, maintain clear financial records, and look credible to clients.

Essential invoice elements

  • Unique invoice number — sequential numbering for tracking
  • Invoice date and due date — when issued and when payment is required
  • Your business info — name, address, contact details, tax ID if applicable
  • Client info — full billing details
  • Itemized line items — description, quantity, rate, amount
  • Subtotal, tax, discount, total — clear financial breakdown
  • Payment instructions — how to pay (bank, check, online)

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I give clients to pay?

Standard is "Net 30" (30 days from invoice date). Faster: Net 15 or "Due upon receipt" for new clients. Slower: Net 60-90 for large enterprises. Build payment terms into your contract before invoicing.

Should I add late fees?

Yes — most states allow 1-2% per month on overdue balances. Mention this on every invoice. Make sure your underlying contract includes the late fee terms (this template assumes you've agreed in advance).

Do I charge sales tax?

Depends on your state and what you're selling. Physical goods almost always taxable. Services vary widely — some states tax all services, others tax none. Check your state's department of revenue.

Can I use this for international clients?

Yes. Add your country/currency, and check if you need to add VAT (Europe) or GST (Canada/Australia). For US-based businesses with foreign clients, ask about W-8BEN forms to avoid US tax withholding.
