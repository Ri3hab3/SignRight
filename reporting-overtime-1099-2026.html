How to Report Qualified Overtime in 2026 (Box 12 Code TT)

Last verified: 31 October 2026 against IRS.gov sources listed below · General information, not tax advice. Confirm with the IRS or a qualified tax professional before you act.

Employers report qualified overtime in W-2 box 12 with code TT, and payers report it for contractors in Form 1099-NEC box 1d. Only the premium part counts, for example the “half” of time-and-a-half.

Status: the IRS has posted the 2026 Form W-2 and the 2026 General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 (issued 29 January 2026). The IRS listed further changes to the 2026 W-2 forms on 19 August 2026, so check the form you actually use. For 1099 forms: the IRS has posted the December 2026 revision of Form 1099-NEC and its instructions, but its own notice says it is still “developing” that revision, and early-release copies are marked “DRAFT — NOT FOR FILING”. We could not confirm a final release, so box numbers, wording or e-file specifications could still change.

Does this apply to me?

  • You are an employer who pays FLSA overtime and issues Forms W-2.
  • You pay contractors and file Form 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC.
  • You are a worker who wants the qualified overtime deduction.
  • You are unsure whether your overtime is FLSA overtime at all.

What counts as qualified overtime

  • Compensation required under section 7 of the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) that is more than the regular rate at which the individual is paid.
  • Only the premium portion is reported. In time-and-a-half, report only the “half”.
  • Overtime is still generally subject to income tax withholding and social security and Medicare taxes.

The worker’s deduction

  • For tax years beginning after 2024 and ending before 2029, individuals can deduct up to $12,500 ($25,000 on a joint return) of qualified overtime compensation.
  • The IRS says qualified overtime must be separately reported on Form W-2 to claim the deduction (FS-2026-13).
  • The amount in box 12 code TT is the total reported, which can be higher than the deduction because of the limit. Other limits can apply; see the IRS FAQs.

What to do next

  1. Employers: map FLSA premium pay to box 12 code TT in payroll.
  2. Payers of contractors: see the new 1099-NEC box 1d.
  3. Workers: keep pay stubs and check your W-2 when it arrives in early 2027.
  4. Read the IRS overtime FAQs (FS-2026-13).

Frequently asked questions

What is box 12 code TT?

The total amount of qualified overtime compensation on Form W-2.

Is all overtime pay reported in TT?

No. Only the part above the regular rate that the FLSA requires. For time-and-a-half, only the extra half.

How much overtime can I deduct?

Up to $12,500, or $25,000 on a joint return, for tax years beginning after 2024 and ending before 2029, subject to other limits in the IRS rules.

What if my W-2 has no code TT?

The IRS says qualified overtime must be separately reported on Form W-2 to claim the deduction. Ask your employer about a corrected W-2c.

Does overtime for contractors count?

Contractors can be covered: Form 1099-NEC has a box 1d for it. Whether pay is FLSA overtime depends on the facts.
