What is a vehicle bill of sale?

A vehicle bill of sale is a written receipt for the private sale of a car, truck, motorcycle or other motor vehicle. It records who sold what to whom, on what date, for how much, and in what condition. It is not the same as the title: the title transfers ownership, while the bill of sale documents the deal. Together they are what most DMVs ask for when the buyer registers the vehicle.

What to include in a car bill of sale

A complete bill of sale for a car or other vehicle should contain:

  • Vehicle details: year, make, model, color and the 17-character VIN (vehicle identification number)
  • Odometer reading: the mileage at the time of sale, and whether it is the actual mileage
  • Sale price and date: the amount paid and the day ownership changed hands
  • Seller and buyer: full legal names, addresses and signatures
  • Condition: usually "As-Is, with no warranty", plus any known problems or title brand (salvage, rebuilt, flood)
  • Declarations: that the seller owns the vehicle and it is free of liens, or details of any lien being paid off

Odometer disclosure: what federal law requires

When ownership of most vehicles is transferred, the seller must state the odometer reading in writing. This federal requirement comes from 49 CFR Part 580. A 2019 NHTSA rule extended the exemption window, so newer vehicles now stay covered for up to 20 years instead of 10; see the Federal Register notice for the details and exact model-year cutoffs.

If the odometer is broken, was replaced, or the true mileage is unknown, say so on the bill of sale. Misstating mileage can lead to civil and criminal penalties, so never leave it blank or guess.

Does a vehicle bill of sale need to be notarized?

In most states the bill of sale does not have to be notarized, but it is not universal. A few states ask for a notary or witnesses on the bill of sale, and others notarize the title assignment instead. Requirements change, so confirm with your state's motor vehicle agency before you meet the other party. Notarizing is optional almost everywhere and adds legal weight if the deal is later disputed. Find your agency through USA.gov's state motor vehicle services directory.

State rules to know before you sell

Every state sets its own rules for registration deadlines and seller paperwork. A few examples from official sources:

California

The DMV offers a bill of sale (REG 135). Sellers must notify the DMV within 5 days of the sale by filing a Notice of Transfer and Release of Liability (REG 138).

Texas

The buyer applies for title with Form 130-U and the signed title within 30 days of purchase. Sellers should also file a Vehicle Transfer Notification (VTR-346) within 30 days. See TxDMV: buying or selling a vehicle.

New York

For private sales the seller signs a Vehicle Bill of Sale (MV-912). Gifts are marked $0 and need a Statement of Transaction (DTF-802). See the NY DMV transfer guide.

Every other state

Use the USA.gov directory to reach your state's DMV, then check its bill of sale, title and registration pages.

What to do after you sign

If you are the seller

  • Sign the title over to the buyer exactly as your state instructs
  • File your state's notice of sale or release of liability right away
  • Remove license plates where required, and cancel or transfer your insurance
  • Keep a signed copy of the bill of sale for your records

If you are the buyer

  • Collect the signed title, the signed bill of sale and any lien release
  • Take them with your ID and proof of insurance to the DMV to title and register the vehicle before your state's deadline
  • Pay sales tax and fees, which are often based on the price on the bill of sale

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving the odometer reading blank or writing a rounded guess
  • Typos in the VIN, or names that do not match the title
  • Writing a lower price to save on tax, which can be treated as fraud
  • Skipping the seller's release of liability and being billed for the buyer's tickets

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a bill of sale required to sell a car?

It depends on the state. Some states require one to register or title a vehicle bought from a private seller; others accept the signed title alone. Even where it is optional, a signed bill of sale is the best proof of the price, date and condition of the sale, so both sides should have one.

What should a vehicle bill of sale include?

The vehicle's year, make, model and VIN; the odometer reading; the sale price and date; the full legal names, addresses and signatures of the buyer and seller; a statement of the vehicle's condition (usually As-Is); and any known title brand such as salvage or rebuilt.

Does a vehicle bill of sale need to be notarized?

Most states do not require the bill of sale itself to be notarized, but a few states, or specific situations in them, do. Rules also differ for the title assignment, which some states notarize. Check your state DMV before you sign, and when in doubt, notarizing adds legal weight at little cost.

Is a bill of sale the same as a title?

No. The title proves who owns the vehicle and is what actually transfers ownership. The bill of sale is the receipt that records the terms of the deal. Most buyers need both to register the vehicle.

What does As-Is mean on a bill of sale?

As-Is means the buyer accepts the vehicle in its current condition with no warranty from the seller. This is standard in private sales. Federal odometer disclosure rules still apply, and the seller must not hide known title problems.

Do I have to disclose the odometer reading?

In most private sales, yes. Federal law (49 CFR Part 580) requires the seller to disclose the mileage when transferring ownership of vehicles that are not exempt. Newer vehicles remain covered for up to 20 years, older ones for 10. If the odometer is broken or the mileage is not actual, say so in writing.

Who keeps the bill of sale?

Both parties should keep a signed copy. The buyer needs it to register the vehicle and to show the price paid for sales tax. The seller needs it as proof the vehicle was sold on a specific date.

Can I use this for a motorcycle, truck, RV or boat?

Yes. The template has a vehicle type selector for cars, trucks, motorcycles, boats and RVs. Boats and trailers can have extra state paperwork, such as a hull identification number or separate registration, so check your state's rules.

Do I need to tell the DMV I sold my car?

Usually yes. Many states require the seller to file a notice of sale or release of liability within a short window, such as 5 days in California or 30 days in Texas. This protects you from tickets, tolls and accidents after the sale.

Can I sell a car as a gift or for $0?

Yes, but write the price as $0 and mark it as a gift. Some states, such as New York, also require a separate form for gifted vehicles. A gift can still affect taxes, so check your state's rules.
