Which template do you actually need?

The most common mistake is reaching for a document that is close but not quite right. A few distinctions worth getting straight before you start:

  • Lease vs. month-to-month. A residential lease locks both parties in for a fixed term, usually a year. A month-to-month agreement rolls over indefinitely and either side can end it with notice. Choose based on how much certainty you want, not on which is shorter.
  • Service agreement vs. contractor agreement. A service agreement defines a project — scope, deliverables, milestones, payment. A contractor agreement additionally addresses worker classification, IP ownership and tax responsibility, which matters when the relationship is ongoing rather than one-off.
  • Bill of sale vs. vehicle bill of sale. Use the general bill of sale for furniture, equipment, jewellery, tools or artwork. Vehicles, boats and trailers need VIN and odometer fields that most DMVs require, so use the dedicated vehicle version.
  • Promissory note vs. loan agreement. A promissory note is a one-sided written promise to repay — simpler, shorter, fine for money lent between people who trust each other. A loan agreement is a two-sided contract covering collateral, default and remedies, and is the right choice for larger sums or anything commercial.

What every template includes

Regardless of which one you choose, each document is structured the same way: identified parties with full legal names and addresses, the specific terms of the arrangement, a governing law clause tied to the US state you select, and a signature block with dated lines for each party. That structure is what makes a document enforceable rather than merely descriptive — a receipt says money changed hands, whereas a properly executed agreement says who agreed to what, when, and under whose law any dispute gets resolved.

Signing and storing your document

Print two copies and have each party sign both, so everyone leaves with an original. Digital signatures are legally recognised across the United States under the ESIGN Act for most everyday agreements — if you would rather sign on screen, run the downloaded PDF through our Sign PDF tool, or use Request eSignature to send it to the other party. Notarisation is not required for the majority of these documents, though for transactions above roughly $500, or anything involving family, it adds meaningful weight for around $5–$25 at most banks and shipping stores.

A note on scope. SignRight provides document templates, not legal advice, and is not a law firm. These templates suit routine, low-conflict situations — a standard tenancy, a freelance project, a private sale between individuals. If there is real money at stake, a dispute already underway, or terms that depart from the ordinary, speak to a licensed attorney in your state before signing anything.
